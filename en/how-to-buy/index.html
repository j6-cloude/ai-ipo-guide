How to buy a US IPO on day one: mechanics, limit orders, broker rules

Last updated: 2026-10-28. Mechanics only; no recommendation to buy or not.

2. How the first trade happens

A Nasdaq IPO has a 10-minute Display-Only period (orders can be entered and cancelled; indicative clearing price and imbalance published every few seconds), then a Pre-Launch period in which the lead underwriter and Nasdaq decide when to run the IPO Cross, a single-price auction, after which continuous trading starts[src][src]. So:

  • The time of the first trade can't be known in advance; the stock usually isn't trading yet at the 9:30 ET open.
  • For Singapore/Hong Kong readers: after Nov 1, 9:30 ET = 22:30 UTC+8, and the first trade may be later. See the converter.

3. Market vs limit orders (how they work)

  • Market order: fills at whatever price is available; no price guarantee. On a volatile first day with wide spreads, the fill can be far from the last price you saw.
  • Limit order: fills only at your price or better, capping what you pay, but may not fill.
  • Many brokers restrict order types on IPO day (for example, only limit orders before the open). Check your broker's notice for the day.

4. Pre-trade checklist (facts to check, not advice)

  1. US trading enabled and funds settled.
  2. FX: converting SGD/HKD/CAD to USD takes time; some brokers auto-convert with a fee.
  3. Whether your broker accepts pre-open orders on IPO day, and which types.
  4. The IPO Cross time is uncertain; watch broker alerts.
  5. Read the S-1 risk factors and lockup terms (see the lockup calendar).
